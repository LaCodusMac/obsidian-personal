### 🔬 What is urobilinogen?
- Urobilinogen is made from **bilirubin**, which comes from the breakdown of **red blood cells** in the liver.
- It’s usually found in small amounts in urine. Low or absent levels can be meaningful.

---

### 🔎 Common causes of **low or absent urobilinogen**:
1. **Bile duct obstruction**  
   - If bile can’t reach the intestines (e.g. due to gallstones or a tumor), bilirubin won’t be converted to urobilinogen.
   - Might also see **pale stools**, **dark urine**, or **jaundice**.

2. **Antibiotic use**  
   - Broad-spectrum antibiotics can kill gut bacteria that help convert bilirubin to urobilinogen.

3. **Severe liver dysfunction**  
   - In some liver diseases (e.g., cirrhosis or late-stage hepatitis), bilirubin metabolism is impaired.

4. **Complete biliary obstruction**  
   - May lead to **zero urobilinogen** in the urine.

---

### ⚠️ Is it serious?
Low levels **alone** are not always concerning. But if it’s accompanied by symptoms like:
- Yellowing of skin/eyes (jaundice)
- Pale or clay-colored stools
- Fatigue
- Itchy skin
- Right upper abdominal pain  
… then you should follow up with a doctor.
