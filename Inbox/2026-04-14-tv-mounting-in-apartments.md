## 🔧 What mounting a TV actually involves

Mounting means:
- Drilling **4–8 holes** into the wall  
- Either hitting **studs** or using heavy-duty anchors  
- Leaving holes that need to be **patched later**

---

## 🏢 What most apartments allow
It usually falls into one of these categories:

### ✅ Allowed (most common)
- Small holes (like nails/screws) are fine  
- You’re expected to **patch them before move-out**

### ⚠️ Conditional
- Mounting is allowed **only into studs**
- Or you may need **written permission**

### ❌ Not allowed (less common but happens)
- Strict “no drilling” lease  
- You risk losing part of your **security deposit**

👉 Check your lease for words like:
- “wall modifications”
- “fixtures”
- “alterations”

## 💡 Real talk (based on your situation)
You already mentioned:
- Your studs might be **24 inches apart**
- Your mount says **max 16 inches**

👉 That matters more than the lease honestly:
- Your current mount **won’t properly hit studs**
- Using only drywall anchors = **higher failure risk**

---

## ⚖️ Your best options (ranked)

### 🥇 Safest (recommended)
- Get a mount that supports **24" stud spacing**
- Drill into studs → strongest + landlord-friendly

### 🥈 Medium risk
- Use **toggle bolts** (heavy-duty anchors)
- Works for lighter TVs, but:
  - More risk over time
  - Landlords sometimes don’t like bigger holes

### 🥉 Zero-risk (no drilling)
- TV stand or floor mount
- No deposit risk at all

---

## 🧠 My honest recommendation for you
Given:
- You’re in an apartment  
- You’ve already had mounting concerns  
- You’re figuring out layout + furniture  

👉 I’d either:
- **Get a proper 24" stud mount**, OR  
- **Use a TV stand for now**

Mounting wrong (especially into drywall only) is one of those things that seems fine… until it isn’t.

---

## 👍 Quick checklist before you mount
- Check lease wording  
- Confirm stud spacing (16 vs 24)  
- Make sure mount supports it  
- Plan how you’ll **patch holes before moving out**
