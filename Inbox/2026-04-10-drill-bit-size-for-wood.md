Can I use a 6mm drill bit for wood or does it have to be 5.5

**ChatGPT:** Short answer: **use 5.5 mm, not 6 mm** (unless you’re in a specific situation).

Here’s why:

- The hole should be **slightly smaller than the screw/lag bolt** so the threads can bite into the wood stud.
- A **6 mm bit makes a bigger hole**, which can reduce grip and weaken the mount—especially important for a TV mount.

### When 6 mm *might* be okay
- If the included bolts are **very thick lag bolts** (like ~8 mm / 5/16")
- Or if the instructions specifically say 6 mm

### What I’d do (safe play)
- Use **5.5 mm pilot hole for wood studs**
- Make sure you’re **dead center in the stud**
- Don’t overtighten (you want tight, not stripped)

**You:** Will stud finder work on wood

**ChatGPT:** Yes — **a stud finder is literally made for finding wood studs behind drywall 👍**

### How it works (simple)
- It detects **density changes** in the wall  
- Drywall = hollow-ish  
- Stud = solid wood → that’s what it locks onto

### How to use it properly (this matters)
1. **Place it flat on the wall**
2. **Turn it on while on the wall** (this calibrates it)
3. Slowly slide left/right
4. It’ll beep/light up when it hits a stud edge

### Pro tips (this is where people mess up)
- Move **slow** — rushing gives false readings  
- Mark **both edges** → stud center is in between  
- Studs are usually **16 inches apart**  
- Avoid areas near outlets at first (can confuse it)

### Quick double-check (highly recommended)
- After marking, **tap the wall**  
  - Hollow sound = no stud  
  - Solid sound = stud  
- Or drill a **tiny pilot hole** to confirm before going big

### One warning
Stud finders can get thrown off by:
- Pipes
- Wiring
- Metal plates
