Why does bottom of oven bacon look like this

**ChatGPT:** That bacon looks **partially cooked rather than spoiled or unsafe**.

A few things are probably happening:

1. **The bottom side wasn't exposed to enough heat**
   - If you baked it on a sheet pan without a rack, the side touching the pan cooks differently.
   - Bacon fat can pool underneath and essentially "confit" the underside instead of crisping it.

2. **Temperature may have been a little low**
   - Bacon cooked at 350°F often stays pink and chewy longer.
   - At 400–425°F the fat renders more completely and the color becomes more uniform.

3. **It's thick-cut bacon**
   - Thick-cut bacon often keeps a pink center even when fully cooked.
   - The color alone isn't a reliable indicator.

From the photo, the bottom strip especially still looks like it has **unrendered fat and some undercooked-looking sections**, while the top pieces appear much closer to done.

### What I'd do
- Put it back in the oven for another **5–10 minutes at 400°F**.
- If it's on a flat pan, flip the strips halfway through.
- Cook until most of the fat turns translucent and the bacon reaches the texture you like.

### Quick check
If you pick up a strip:
- **Soft, floppy, and raw-looking fat** → needs more time.
- **Flexible but cooked, with rendered fat** → safe, just chewy.
- **Crisp edges and mostly rendered fat** → fully done/crispy.

Based on this picture alone, I'd give it **another few minutes in the oven** before eating it.
