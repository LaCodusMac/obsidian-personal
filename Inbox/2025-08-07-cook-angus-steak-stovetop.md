**ChatGPT:** Here’s a simple way to cook an Angus steak on the stovetop so it comes out juicy with a nice crust:  

---

**1. Pick your steak & let it rest**  
- Take your steak (ribeye, sirloin, NY strip, etc.) out of the fridge about **30 minutes** before cooking so it cooks evenly.  
- Pat it **completely dry** with paper towels — moisture kills sear.  

**2. Season generously**  
- Coat with **kosher salt and freshly cracked black pepper** on both sides.  
- Optional: add a light brush of oil or a dusting of garlic powder or smoked paprika for extra flavor.  

**3. Heat your pan**  
- Use a **heavy skillet** (cast iron is best).  
- Heat over **medium-high** until it’s very hot — about 3–4 minutes.  
- Add a high smoke-point oil (canola, avocado, or grapeseed) just enough to coat the bottom.  

**4. Sear the steak**  
- Place the steak in the pan **away from you** to avoid splatter.  
- Don’t move it for **2–4 minutes** (depends on thickness) until you get a deep brown crust.  
- Flip once and sear the other side for another **2–4 minutes**.  

**5. Add flavor (optional but amazing)**  
- Reduce heat to medium.  
- Add **2–3 tbsp butter**, **crushed garlic cloves**, and **fresh herbs** (rosemary or thyme).  
- Tilt the pan slightly and spoon the melted butter over the steak for 1–2 minutes (“basting”).  

**6. Check doneness** (internal temp guide):  
- Rare: 120–125°F  
- Medium-rare: 130–135°F  
- Medium: 140–145°F  
- Medium-well: 150–155°F  
- Use a meat thermometer for best accuracy.  

**7. Rest before slicing**  
- Transfer to a plate or cutting board, tent loosely with foil, and rest **5–10 minutes**. This locks in juices.
