## 🧴 What “cleaning vinegar” does
- It’s stronger than regular vinegar (~6% acetic acid vs ~5%)
- Cuts through **light–moderate grease**, mineral buildup, and grime
- Works best on **non-porous surfaces**

---

## 🛠️ How to use it for degreasing

### 🔹 1. Basic degreaser spray
- Mix:
  - 1 part cleaning vinegar  
  - 1 part warm water  
- Optional: add a few drops of dish soap (this is key for grease)

👉 Spray → let sit 2–5 minutes → wipe

---

### 🔹 2. Heavy grease (stove / backsplash)

- Spray straight (undiluted) vinegar  
- Let it sit 5–10 minutes  
- Scrub with sponge or microfiber  
- Wipe clean

💡 **Pro move:** follow with a tiny bit of dish soap + hot water if grease is stubborn

---

### 🔹 3. Cabinets / sticky residue
- Mix vinegar + a drop of dish soap
- Use a damp cloth (not soaking—protect wood)
- Dry right after

---

## ⚠️ What NOT to use vinegar on
This matters more than people think:
- ❌ Granite / marble (etching)
- ❌ Natural stone
- ❌ Unsealed wood
- ❌ Some electronics screens

---

## 🔥 Real talk (so you don’t waste time)
- Vinegar alone = **okay degreaser**
- Vinegar + dish soap = **actually effective**
- For **thick baked-on grease**, something like a dedicated degreaser will beat vinegar

---

## 💡 Simple “best mix” (what I’d actually use)

- Splash of vinegar  
- Few drops dish soap  

That combo hits:
- grease (soap)
